1. Topic map

Academic reading is a coordinated task.

A strong reader changes strategy according to the purpose. Some texts require close argument analysis, some require rapid retrieval of a value, and some require comparison across several documents.

3.3 Reading & viewing skills

Key idea. Critical reading is not automatic disagreement. It is disciplined interpretation in which claims, evidence, language, source and relationships are examined before a conclusion is accepted.

Reference exampleWhat does “reading across texts” look like in research?

Trust Matters

Bråten, Strømsø and Britt (2009) asked students to read seven texts about global warming that differed in source characteristics. Students' source evaluations were related to how well they constructed meaning across the documents.

Try it: Imagine two texts make the same claim, but one is an identified scientific report and the other is an anonymous web post. Should both claims receive equal weight? Explain what you would verify before deciding.

Open Bråten et al. (2009) ↗

Multiple-text use as a process

List and Alexander (2019) describe multiple-text use as a process involving preparation, execution and production. This is useful when you move from deciding what a task requires, to reading and connecting sources, to producing a response.

Try it: Before reading three articles, write one synthesis question. While reading, record agreements and conflicts. After reading, build one conclusion that explicitly reflects those relationships.

Peer review: quality control, not a truth certificate

Peer review is an important scholarly quality-control process. A credible journal should explain its review policy clearly. Even after peer review, the reader still needs to inspect the article itself.

1
Process
Is peer review described transparently?
2
Evidence
Does the article show defensible methods and data?
3
Scope
Are limitations and uncertainty preserved?

Verify “Scopus indexed” directly

Never treat a badge on a journal website as sufficient evidence. Search the exact journal title or ISSN in the official Scopus Sources interface, then check the matching source record and coverage information.

Do not infer current status from memory. Indexing can change over time. Verify the current record when the decision matters.

Quartile Explorer

Click a band. The important question is not only “Which quartile?”, but “Which metric, category and year?”

Select Q1, Q2, Q3 or Q4.
Myth. Quartile is a journal-level contextual indicator. You still need to evaluate the individual article.
Myth. Quartile position and deceptive publishing are different questions. A lower quartile does not establish predatory conduct.
Myth. SJR and CiteScore are distinct journal-metric systems. Always name the system, category and year.

Predatory or deceptive publishing: look for a pattern

A single red flag is not a verdict. Concern increases when several deceptive or non-transparent practices appear together.

Indexing

False or misleading indexing claims, invented metrics, or badges that cannot be independently verified.

Review

Implausibly fast or unclear peer review, guaranteed acceptance, or no explanation of the editorial process.

Transparency

Hidden or changing fees, unverifiable editorial board information, unclear ownership or contact details.

Important caution. Open access, APCs, a new journal, Q4 status, or non-Scopus status do not by themselves prove that a journal is predatory.

5. Multi-text synthesis lab

Do not stack summaries. Build relationships.

A synthesis explains what sources collectively show, where they differ and how much each source changes the overall conclusion.

Verification example

A journal says “Indexed in Scopus”. What do you actually do?

  1. Copy the journal's exact title and ISSN.
  2. Search the official Scopus Sources record, not the journal's own badge.
  3. Check publisher, subject area and coverage years. If coverage is discontinued, do not describe current issues as automatically covered.
  4. If you quote a quartile, state the system, year and subject category. Do not turn a journal-level quartile into an article-level quality score.
